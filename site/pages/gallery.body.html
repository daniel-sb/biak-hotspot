Three of the 2026 season's events have both hand-drawn interpretation boxes and 1 m Sentinel-2 imagery. They are the three the validated area figure covers, and the three this atlas draws. Every other event on the front page is mapped from 20 m bands and has no plate here, which is a limit of the imagery, not a judgement about those fires.

Each plate puts the mapped burn polygon over the false-colour scene that produced it, with the interpretation boxes, the thermal anomalies detected while the event was running, and a strip of true-colour frames across six dates. One contrast stretch is applied to all six: stretching each date against its own histogram would brighten the burn straight back out of the series.

How these were laid out. The plan was to compose them in Layout Composer, the cartographic layout plugin for GeoLibre. That plugin is a hand-driven ribbon editor inside the GeoLibre desktop app with no command line, so no script can call it. These plates carry the same anatomy — map frame, locator inset, date series, scale bar, north arrow, legend, title block — rendered from the data by scripts/burn_atlas.py, so each one is reproducible and every number on it traces to a file in the repository. Each plate also ships a GeoLibre project holding the same layers at the same extent, so the hand-finished route stays open: open it in GeoLibre, enable Layout Composer, and the frame is already set.

What a plate does not say

The hectares on a plate are a mapped extent: the area the dNBR+ product drew inside the event's footprint. That figure has no interval, and it is not the validated area. The validated estimate on the results page covers these three footprints together and comes out below what the product maps across them. Neither number says who lit anything, and a burn scar in an image is not evidence of an offence.
