    What a hotspot is — and is not. A hotspot is a thermal anomaly detected by a satellite sensor. It is not a confirmed fire, and it cannot identify who started anything: a 375 m pixel cannot attribute burning to any person, landowner or village. Small-scale shifting cultivation is a long-standing and lawful practice in Papua; a monitoring product that frames every detection as illegal misrepresents the data. False-positive caveats are published with the counts, every time.

    These two area figures are not the same quantity. One is what the product drew; the other is what a sample of hand-interpreted 1 m imagery says actually burned inside three footprints. Neither is a count of fires, and nothing here says who lit anything. Read how these numbers were made and what they do and do not support before quoting any of them.

    Observation gap. Nothing observes Biak between 15:00 and 00:31 WIT. Himawari-9 covers those hours from geostationary orbit at 2 km resolution — 28 times the pixel area of VIIRS — so it sees only larger or hotter fires. The daily brief carries each evening's result.
