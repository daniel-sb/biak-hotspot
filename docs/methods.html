How these numbers were made

Thermal anomalies detected from satellite around Biak, Supiori and Numfor, Papua, Indonesia. Data: NASA FIRMS (VIIRS and MODIS) and Himawari-9.

    This page is in English only. Its readers are reviewers, and translating the sensor-level detail buys nobody anything.

    Detections

    Thermal anomalies come from NASA FIRMS: VIIRS on Suomi NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 at 375 m, and MODIS on Terra and Aqua at 1 km. Timestamps are stored in UTC and a WIT (UTC+9) column is derived from them; every daily aggregation is on WIT local days, because treating a night overpass as the previous day shifts detections into the wrong date.

    The confidence column is published as the raw instrument code and is never cast to a number. VIIRS returns l, n or h and MODIS returns 0 to 100 in the same field, so the two are not comparable and a numeric cast would silently discard most rows. Confidence is never used to size or colour anything on the map; that encoding is Fire Radiative Power.

    Quality control adds boolean flag columns. It never deletes rows, so every filter is reversible and auditable, and an offshore or over-water detection is kept and flagged rather than dropped.

    A failed fetch and an empty response are different facts and stay distinguishable in the stored output and in the exit code. A day with no detections and a day we failed to retrieve never render as the same thing.

    Events

    Detections are clustered into events by DBSCAN on distance and time. An event carries a first-seen and last-seen WIT timestamp, a detection count, an overpass count and FRP statistics. Events whose detections come from a single overpass are flagged as singletons, because one overpass cannot establish duration.

    An event is a cluster of thermal anomalies. It is not a confirmed fire, and the word fire is reserved for episodes corroborated by an independent source.

    Burned area: the mapped extent

    Burned area is mapped with dNBR+, following Alcaras et al. (2022), on Sentinel-2 Level-2A. NBR+ is (B12 − B8A − B3 − B2) / (B12 + B8A + B3 + B2): subtracting the blue and green bands sends water dark and pushes cloud negative, which is why this index refuses the sea where a plain NBR does not.

    Scene selection is per pixel, not per scene. For each pixel the product takes the latest usable observation before the event started, within 60 days, and the earliest after it ended, within 30 days, keeping only observations where Cloud Score+ cs_cdf ≥ 0.6. Those are search windows, not averaging windows: no composite is formed. The lag between each pixel's observation and the event is recorded, and the before side is systematically staler than the after side, because the search runs backwards into the cloudier build-up.

    The threshold is local, not global: a pixel is called changed when its dNBR+ exceeds the 99th percentile of never-detected land in a reference ring around the same event. This controls false alarms on ground that never burned. It is not an accuracy, and light burns below the local noise are missed.

    Burned area: the validated estimate

    The product was measured against hand labels on 1 m imagery reconstructed by S2DR4 (Gamma Earth). Sample boxes of 60 m were drawn in three strata: boxes where the product claims burning, boxes inside an event footprint where it claims nothing, and boxes drawn at random within the footprint. Each box was read as burned, unburned or unsure by one interpreter, with a stated confidence. Unsure boxes are dropped from the estimate rather than forced into a class.

    Area is then estimated by stratified estimation following Olofsson et al. (2014), with the per-stratum rates carrying Wilson intervals rather than the normal approximation. The normal approximation gives a stratum with an extreme rate zero variance, which produces an interval narrower than the sample can support; the published interval is the wider, honest one. Strata are never pooled.

    One caution on the imagery, recorded because it produced a wrong result before it was caught: the S2DR4 reconstruction returns its bands in the order B2, B3, B4, B8, B5, B6, B7, B11, B12, B8A, and the file carries no band descriptions. Reading those as an ordinary Sentinel-2 stack puts the wrong wavelengths into every index. Rendered chips are already 8-bit RGB and must not be stretched again.

    What is published, and what is held back

    Detection coordinates are published, because a located thermal anomaly is an observation. Inferred responsibility is never published: a 375 m pixel cannot attribute burning to a person, a landowner or a village, and small-scale shifting cultivation is lawful and long-established in Papua.

    Burn polygons are published at native resolution with no attribute table beyond the event they belong to, and no join to land ownership or land use. Counts are aggregated to distrik. The interpretation boxes are published as a map layer with their stratum, judgement and interpreter confidence; the free-text notes written during interpretation are not published. Modelled fuel is published only as a distrik aggregate, because a fuel class is an inference about a place rather than an observation of it.

    Sources

    • NASA FIRMS, VIIRS and MODIS active fire products.
    • Himawari-9 AHI, JAXA P-Tree, for the 15:00 to 00:31 WIT window nothing else observes.
    • Copernicus Sentinel-2 Level-2A, with Cloud Score+ for observation quality.
