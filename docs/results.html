    Two area figures, two different questions

    The project publishes two burned-area numbers and they are not alternatives. One is what the detection-driven product drew across the season. The other is what hand interpretation of 1 m imagery says actually burned, inside three event footprints, with an interval. The second is not a correction of the first, because it does not cover the same ground.

    The estimate against the product, on one axis

    The product's mapped extent falls outside the interval, below it. That is the finding: inside the ground we labelled, the product maps less than burned. The gap is omission, and it is not small.

    How the sample was stratified

    The estimate is stratified because the two kinds of ground behave nothing alike. Where the product claims burning it is nearly always right. Where it claims nothing, inside a footprint, it is wrong about half the time. Pooling those two would hide exactly the thing worth knowing, so the strata are never pooled.

    What these numbers do not support

    • They are not a count of fires. A detection is a thermal anomaly in a satellite pixel. The events on the front page are clusters of detections, not confirmed fires, and nothing here identifies a cause or a person.
    • The validated figure does not scale to the season. Multiplying the season's mapped extent by the ratio between these two numbers would be an extrapolation from three footprints to twenty-five events, and nobody has tested whether it holds.
    • One interpreter produced the labels. Agreement between interpreters was never measured, because no second interpreter was available. That is a permanent limitation of this sample, not a gap waiting to be filled.
    • The sampling unit is a 60 m box, not a pixel. A box is a cluster of pixels treated as one judgement, which is why the interval is wider than a pixel count would suggest.
    • Events the product could not assess are excluded, not zero. Where no usable local reference ring existed, the event carries no area. That is a missing measurement.
